Who we are

A better city is something we can build together.

Children Xplanet is a civic innovation organization that gives children and young people meaningful opportunities to study their communities and contribute to solving the problems that shape everyday life.

🌱

Our mission

To empower young people to improve their cities and their planet through environmental action, scientific inquiry, engineering, technology, and community collaboration—while nurturing the creativity, empathy, and judgment that make us human. We turn local challenges into hands-on learning experiences that prepare children for the AI age and create measurable public benefits.

Our project portfolio

Houston is our classroom. The city is our laboratory.

Our projects connect environmental improvement with civic technology. Some begin with a tree or a water tank. Others begin with a map, a dataset, or a question about how city infrastructure works.

  • First flagship initiative

    Urban Tree Innovation Challenge

    Students research where Houston needs trees most, design low-cost systems to help young trees survive, and bring the best ideas into real community planting projects.

  • Urban infrastructure

    Sewer System Health Map

    A public map of sewer asset conditions, failures, and maintenance needs.

  • Climate resilience

    Houston Flooding & Drainage Observatory

    Maps recurring flood spots and drainage issues to target green infrastructure.

  • Urban climate

    Neighborhood Heatmap & Shade Equity

    Measures how canopy, pavement, and land cover affect neighborhood temperatures.

  • Public information

    Houston Road Health Portal

    A public map of potholes, road conditions, and repair history.

  • Mobility and safety

    Traffic & Safer Streets Lab

    Studies congestion, dangerous intersections, and safer routes for pedestrians and cyclists.

  • Water innovation

    Water Reuse & Smart Irrigation

    Prototypes low-cost rainwater harvesting and efficient watering for urban greening.

  • Healthy neighborhoods

    Urban Food Trees & Community Harvest

    Explores publicly accessible fruit trees for shade, food, and community gathering.

  • Public realm

    Neighborhood Beauty & Biodiversity

    Studies native plants, pollinator habitats, and ways to make public spaces welcoming.

  • Open civic data

    Houston Urban Data Atlas

    A long-term platform combining maps and student research on urban challenges.

  • Future innovation

    Student Solutions Incubator

    An open call for students to propose and test new local solutions.
