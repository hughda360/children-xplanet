The challenge

Plant trees where they are needed most.

Many Houston neighborhoods need more shade and green space. Some public sites may also lack convenient water or power connections, making tree establishment difficult.

Our goal

Children Xplanet brings students together to find suitable public planting locations, identify trees that fit Houston's climate, and develop practical ways to establish them with limited resources.

The project connects urban forestry, environmental science, water conservation, engineering, and community service.

Our central question

How can we help a young tree survive in a challenging urban location without relying on municipal water or grid electricity? Students are encouraged to use AI as a learning and research assistant—while checking sources, testing ideas, and explaining their own conclusions.

A three-phase project

Learn. Compete. Make an impact.

The project moves from research to hands-on engineering, then into community use.

PHASE 01 · RESEARCH

Study the city

Students investigate where trees are most needed and which species can provide the greatest environmental benefit.

  • Map low-canopy and high-heat areas.
  • Identify public land and water access.
  • Compare native and climate-adapted trees.
  • Learn through AI, public data, and expert guidance.
PHASE 02 · ENGINEERING

Design and compete

Teams build low-cost solutions and test them on comparable trees and sites.

  • Summer: passive irrigation challenge.
  • Winter: passive cold-protection challenge.
  • Use recycled materials and practical technology.
  • Measure cost, performance, and tree health.
PHASE 03 · COMMUNITY

Put winning ideas to work

Promising designs are improved, documented, and adapted for larger volunteer planting events.

  • Refine and simplify winning designs.
  • Publish instructions and real costs.
  • Work with public agencies and communities.
  • Track tree survival and long-term impact.
Phase 02 · Student competitions

Two seasons. Two engineering challenges.

Teams work independently, with shared rules and comparable starting conditions. Each challenge runs for three months after installation.

Summer competition

Passive Irrigation Challenge

Design a low-cost system that keeps a newly planted tree healthy with no routine human care.

  • All teams plant the same tree species at the same site.
  • No municipal water or grid electricity.
  • Recycled containers, rainwater collection, gravity-fed delivery, timers, chips, batteries, and approved solar devices may be used.
  • All equipment must fit within a 1.5-meter radius of the tree.
  • Teams have one hour to plant and install their systems.
  • No manual watering, refilling, repairs, or adjustments after installation.
Winter competition

Passive Cold-Protection Challenge

Develop an affordable way to protect a selected young tree from Houston winter cold and occasional freezes.

  • All teams use the same selected tree species and comparable seedlings.
  • Design passive insulation and root or trunk protection.
  • Use low-cost, reusable, and environmentally responsible materials.
  • No grid-powered heating or routine human intervention.
  • Equipment must fit within a 1.5-meter radius.
  • Teams have one hour to plant and install their systems.
  • Record temperatures, freeze exposure, and tree condition.

Fair testing

Use comparable seedlings, site conditions, planting procedures, and starting resources.

Tree welfare first

Independent monitors check tree health. Emergency intervention is allowed and recorded.

Safe public projects

Obtain landowner approval, check underground utilities, and follow environmental and site rules.
